Reading the pages

 The drawn melody Every word is colored and placed by its pitch relative to that speaker's own voice: red = high, ink = mid, blue = low — and the word physically sits higher or lower on the line. Read a sentence the way you'd read sheet music.
Because contrast gives it shape.  “contrast” peaks high, “it” sits low
st — semitones The unit of melody: one piano key. 12 st = one octave. A sentence's “sweep” is the distance from its lowest to highest pitch. Flat conversational English lives around 5–7 st; Geoff's median is 10; his best sentences sweep 20+, nearly two octaves.
16.8 st ≈ an octave and a third — sing do→mi′ on a piano and that's the ground this one sentence covers.
Hz & note names The sweep's actual endpoints as frequency and piano note, so you know where the melody lives, not just how wide it is. Geoff's register centers near 105 Hz (about G2–A2) — an octave below a typical female voice.
76–181 Hz · D#2→F#3 = from a bass fry bottom up past the middle of a male voice. D#2 is near the lowest playable cello note.
hill & floor The highest and lowest word of the sentence, named so you know exactly where to aim the peak and the dive when you shadow it. The number is that word's distance from the sentence's own average.
hill on “twice.” (+11 st) · floor on “this” (−1 st) → plan the sentence around lifting “twice” one octave.
end ± dB — the landing How loud the final words arrive compared with the middle of the sentence. Positive = the ending holds or grows (authority — nothing tapers); negative = the ending decays. Most untrained speech falls off 8–14 dB.
end +8.2 dB = the last word is the strongest thing in the sentence · end −12 dB = the thought audibly dies out.
wpm — pace Words per minute for that sentence. The point of the study: pace and melody are independent dials — Geoff stays melodic at every speed.
130 wpm = deliberate teaching · 256 = his median · 449 = full sprint, still sweeping 12 st.
★ model sentence & strength labels In the sentence study, each strength (signpost, fry floor, final drop, held landing…) is a selectable label. The ★ card is the curated exemplar; the rows under it are more of the same move, mined from the whole corpus.
