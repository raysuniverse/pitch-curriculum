Pitch Apprenticeship — the nine patterns, clips & Geoff's demos

The performer clips Geoff chose, each with his own demo(s). Shadow the clip, then compare with Geoff's rendering. Melodic range in semitones; timestamps refer to the source video.
All patterns at a glance
  1. Pattern 1 · March-down — One early stress, then many lows in a row — the default sentence shape; keep pushing lower even when you think you're low.
  2. Pattern 2 · The U (high-low-low-rise) — High start, low middle, rise at the end — the mid-thought signal: 'I'm not done talking.'
  3. Pattern 3 · Combo punch (heavy, then heavier) — A heavy stress followed by an even heavier one — both words matter, the second lands harder.
  4. Pattern 4 · Final-word rise — Last word stressed with rising intonation — openness, invitation, or edge/sarcasm.
  5. Pattern 5 · Repeated pattern (parallel lists) — The same melody replayed item after item — the sound of a list.
  6. Pattern 6 · Three-word group (heavy-weak-heavy) — Bookend stress with a weak middle: REALLY really imPORTANT.
  7. Pattern 7 · Heavy stress + floor-drop ending — Heavy stress, then the final syllables dive extra low — the adjective is the takeaway.
  8. Pattern 8 · Punchy heavy-fall — Firm falling finals (Mandarin-4th-tone-like) — authority and attitude; a rise would soften it.
  9. Pattern 9 · Soft-rise ending — Same skeleton as pattern 8 but a gentle rise — softens and invites instead of punching.
How to practice a patternLook at the drawn melody (red = high, blue = low, word height = pitch) and find the hill and floor words → hum the shape without words → ⟳ loop the clip and speak over it → record yourself and compare. Exaggerate: your “too much” usually lands at native-normal.
