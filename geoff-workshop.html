The performer clips Geoff chose, each with his own demo(s). Shadow the clip, then compare with Geoff's rendering. Melodic range in semitones; timestamps refer to the source video.
All patterns at a glance
  1. High pitch, then step down (storytelling) — Start the story HIGH, then step each following thought group down.
  2. Ending shift-up — The final thought group steps HIGHER than the one before it.
  3. Pattern disrupt (floors 3-2-5) — Two parallel groups lull the ear; the third breaks the pattern with a jump (and a pause + speed change).
  4. Stacked highs + compound stress (MIC stand) — A high thought group followed by an even higher one; compound nouns stress the FIRST word (MIC stand).
  5. Clarification wobble — Low dragged intro ('you mean…'), then the target word high with a wavering note.
  6. Dialogue intro drop 1 — The quote-introducer ('I was like') sits LOW; the quoted speech JUMPS high.
  7. Steps in a process — Each step gets its own floor, mostly climbing; only one group steps down.
  8. Dialogue intro drop 2 — Same move, second speaker: low tag, high quote — and a voice/placement change for the character.
  9. Contrast + 2-second drag — A long dragged word FULL of movement keeps the energy alive; don't rush it.
  10. Waver + cut for energy — Waver on the key word; CUT the other syllables short — dragging saps energy, cutting adds urgency.
  11. List melody (fillers sit low) — Each list item on its own level; the 'uh, you know' filler sits low on purpose.
  12. Mocking emphasis (every syllable clear) — Slow down and articulate EVERY syllable of the key word ('coun-ted').
  13. Repetition reset — Repeat the false start: first time low and dragged, the repeat JUMPS high.
  14. Clarifying restatement — Re-say the point with the new word high ('my FINANCIAL lane').
How to practice a patternLook at the drawn melody (red = high, blue = low, word height = pitch) and find the hill and floor words → hum the shape without words → ⟳ loop the clip and speak over it → record yourself and compare. Exaggerate: your “too much” usually lands at native-normal.
